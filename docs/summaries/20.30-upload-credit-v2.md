## The problem in one paragraph
Wisp CONTINUE is absolute (the client sets its credit to the value, wisp-js and epoxy alike). The server refills at
half, so on a long path several CONTINUEs are in flight; the server cannot tell which ones the client overwrote, so
the credit it must treat as legal (`allow`) grows without limit (20.27: 66-372 MiB at 200 ms). Behind a target that
stalls, all of that lands in `Target::pend`. base-1 killed honest streams with CLOSE 0x49 at PEND_HARD (64 MiB);
base-2 instead holds the whole client connection (`Client::hpaused`, 4-6 s at most) once an in-credit stream passes
PEND_HARD. Separately, echo 10x10 at level 0 keeps 4-5 MiB of pend per stream (PEND_LOW + one 128-packet grant +
what the client sent before the CONTINUE arrived): 317 MiB VmHWM on VPS3, ~95% of it pend.

## Cheap interim: PEND_HARD scaled to the stream window (evaluated, rejected)
Idea (60.12): hold at k x the stream's window instead of a flat 64 MiB. What it changes:
- A1/A4/A5: the in-flight data that lands in pend is set by the client's buffers and the ghost credit (66-372 MiB of
  `allow` measured, diag-0x49), not by our window. A lower threshold holds MORE often and earlier, i.e. it pauses the
  sibling streams (A5) more, and A4 gets worse. It does not lower what arrives, only where we stop reading.
- A3: echo pend is 4-5 MiB per stream, far below any threshold that does not hold honest WAN uploads; echo memory
  does not move.
- A2: unchanged at best (the throughput comes from the over-commit; 20.27: any rule that keeps pend small cut 1x1
  behind a stalling sink to 0.61-0.64).
So it buys nothing on the bar items it was meant for and costs A4/A5. Not built. (Reasoning from the measured
numbers in 20.27 and diag-0x49; no new run.)

## Design v2 (proposed)
Two parts, both at level 0 only (levels 1+ keep the 20.26 r4 stage grants: already byte-bounded and small).

**Part 1, the hard part: retire overwritten credit with a WS ping after the CONTINUE.**
- When a flush carries CONTINUEs and the client has no ping outstanding, append a WS ping (payload = 8-byte seq)
  after them. One ping per client at a time (at most one per RTT). CONTINUEs pushed while a ping is outstanding
  belong to the next epoch; the pong of epoch e sends the next ping if CONTINUEs wait.
- Clients answer pings in order of receipt (RFC 6455 5.5.2; wbench, ws (node), browsers, tungstenite/fastwebsockets
  auto-pong): every client DATA packet that reaches us after pong(e) was sent after the client received the CONTINUEs
  of epoch e. (A browser may run JS that still spends the OLD credit between receipt and processing the CONTINUE
  message: gap G, see the spike.)
- Per stream: `lim` = the absolute packet count (in `since` units since stream start) the client may legally reach.
  On pong(e) for a stream whose last CONTINUE value n is in epoch e: lim = recv_at_pong + G + n (all earlier
  remainders retired: they were overwritten). CONTINUEs of later epochs, not yet ponged, are added (worst case each is
  received and fully used before the next): bound = lim + sum(unacked n). This is the exact bound up to G, instead of
  `next_allow`'s ever-growing sum.
- Grants become top-up: a CONTINUE value n is chosen so that bound - target_consumed <= W_s (the stream window in
  packets of the mean size), i.e. pend can never legally exceed W_s + one batch. No refill-at-half chains.
- A client that never pongs (or a proxy that eats pings): no retirement -> after a timeout (e.g. 2 x the client's
  TCP RTT + 1 s) fall back to the base-2 rules for that client (refill at half, hold as safety net). Behaviour for
  such clients is unchanged, not worse.
- The hold (Client::hpaused at PEND_HARD) stays as the safety net for over-credit or unseen-overwrite clients; with
  W_s <= PEND_HARD / 2 an honest ponging client cannot reach it (A4, A5).

**Part 2: the per-stream window W_s (bytes), autotuned like TCP's receive buffer.**
- RTT = the ping round trip (through any proxy), min-filtered; before the first sample: the client socket's
  tcpi_rtt.
- Grow: W_s = max(W_s, 2 x bytes written to the target in the last RTT), only while pend was empty at the refill
  (the target keeps up). Never shrink on a stall (a pause does not mean a smaller window is needed: shrinking is what
  cost 20.27's busy cut 36-39% on A2). Clamp to [W_min, W_max]: W_min = 512 KiB (and >= 8 packets), W_max = 32 MiB
  (= PEND_HARD / 2; 200 ms x 1 Gbit = 25 MB BDP).
- Echo on loopback: the target is the bottleneck (pend > 0) and the RTT is < 1 ms, so W_s stays near W_min: pend per
  stream ~ W_min + PEND_LOW instead of 4-5 MiB (A3: 100 streams ~75 MiB of pend vs ~400).
- Open risk for A2: base-2 reaches ~45 MiB/s behind the stalling sink with ~60 MiB of pend (over-commit). W_max 32 MiB
  may be below that; the spike measures A2 with W_max 32 and 48 MiB before anything else is built.

## Spike results (2026-10-27, branch flow-spike, NOT for merge)
Code: flow-spike 835dd15 (worker.rs, `SpClient`/`SpTarget`, `spike_pong`, the `v2` branch of `after_write`).
FW_CREDIT_SPIKE=1 = S1 accounting only; FW_CREDIT_V2=1 = S2 grants (level 0 only, after the first pong).

**S1 (retirement, instrumentation only): PASS.**
- Every client tested answers the server's WS ping with the same payload: wbench, wisp-js 0.5.0 (node, v1),
  epoxy simple-wisp-client (v1 and v2). 0 violations of the retired bound in every run (laptop loopback upload 1x1,
  10x10, echo 2x4/10x10; VPS2 rig A 1x1 x 8 runs).
- The retired bound is tight: max(bound - recv) 127-882 packets, against allow - since 13k-693k packets
  (the ghost credit that base-2 must treat as legal).
- Catch: the bound over-estimates an EAGER client (wisp-js sends its queue inside the CONTINUE handler, before it
  answers the ping) by up to one CONTINUE. Used alone for grants it deadlocks (wisp-js compat echo stalled at 6/8 MiB).
  Fix in S2: a liveness probe (below). The kill/hold side can use the bound as is (it is an upper bound).

**S2 grant rule (as spiked).**
- Top-up: grant n = room / pkt when room = win - backlog - (bound - recv) x pkt >= win/4.
- Liveness probe: when no grant is due but the client has sent its last CONTINUE's worth since we pushed it,
  arm a ping; if its pong comes back with no DATA for that stream, grant as if the client had no credit
  (`gblocked`). Bounded: the client then has at most old remaining + n <= 2 x win.
- Window: start 1 MiB; grow to 2 x (bytes consumed per path RTT) when >= half the window moved in one RTT, the target
  kept up (backlog < PEND_LOW) and the ping RTT is not inflated (<= 1.25 x tcpi_min_rtt + 5 ms). Never shrinks.
  Clamped to W_max per stream AND per connection (sum of its streams; one TCP pipe needs one BDP).
- Two dead ends on the way: (1) a "client reached the bound" growth signal never fires (the bound over-estimates);
  (2) RTT from our own pings is inflated by our downstream queue and by a busy server (5-40 ms on loopback echo,
  even the first sample), so windows grew to 3-30 MiB per echo stream. The kernel's tcpi_min_rtt fixes both.

**What the numbers say.**
- The retirement works and is cheap; it removes the ghost credit for every client we have.
- A3 (echo memory) is the big win: ~0.12 of base on loopback (needs the cgroup run on VPS2, n >= 8).
- A1/A2 hold at W_max 56 (no kill, throughput tie-or-better, memory 0.87). W_max 32 saves memory but costs throughput.
- A5 is NOT shown: base-2 never held in the A5 runs (the A5 client sends exactly within credit, so its pend stays
  below PEND_HARD). The hold needs a client with a big send buffer (wbench, 96 MiB wmem). The A5 test must use such a
  client (or wbench with 2 targets) to measure what it was meant to measure.
- New regression found: a single honest stream to a FAST sink at 200 ms is window-bound under v2 (0.67 / 0.90).
  Cause: one retirement per RTT, and grants only when room >= win/4. Needs a fix before A6 can pass
  (candidate: grant from consumption since the last pong, not from the retired bound, while backlog is low).

## Implementation (branch credit-v2, 2026-10-28)
Lead GO (W_max 56 MiB, flag first, default off; the default flips only after the full bar AND a user OK).
Code: server/src/credit.rs (`Pinger` per connection, `Stream` per TCP stream; pure, unit-tested), worker.rs hooks
(`push_continue`, `credit_pong`, DATA -> `Stream::on_data`, the v2 branch of `after_write`, the sweep's liveness net),
net::min_rtt_us (tcpi_min_rtt), cli `--credit-v2`. Off: `Client::cv` / `Target::cv` are None (one Option check per
DATA packet; no ping, no allocation).

Rule (level 0, client answered >= 1 ping, no ping outstanding > PONG_LATE 5 s; else base-2 / stage grants):
- bound = recv at the last retired pong + its epoch's last CONTINUE + CONTINUEs of unretired epochs (every CONTINUE,
  base or v2, is recorded). `viol` counts packets past it; nothing enforces it (closes still use base `allow`).
- grant n = min((win - backlog - min(rest x pkt, est)) / pkt, (cap - backlog - rest x pmax) / pmax), rest = bound - recv,
  est = bytes received per ping RTT (between the last two pongs), cap = PEND_HARD - 1/16 (60 MiB), win >= 2 x pmax.
  Due when n x pkt >= win/8 and (a step win/16, >= 1 MiB, <= 3/4 win, was consumed or the last grant is spent).
- Probe: spent, nothing due, rest > 0: a ping; if its pong comes with no DATA for the stream, it is `blocked` (rest 0).
- Window 1 MiB -> 56 MiB: 2 x consumption per kernel min RTT plus the tcp_rcv_space_adjust growth term (up to 6x while
  the rate doubles), only while >= half the window moved per RTT and the target kept up. Never shrinks.
- Sweep (2 s): re-runs after_write for v2 streams (late-pong fallback and level changes have no other wakeup).

Fast-sink regression (#2) and what fixed it (each step measured; none changed the protocol):
1. in-flight estimate instead of the bound's rest (the bound counts dropped credit of overwriting clients);
2. grant per consumed step, not at half of the last grant;
3. growth term (2x alone kept the window at cwnd and stalled slow start: 200 ms 0.94x -> 0.979x);
4. no "uninflated RTT" growth gate (the pong queues behind the client's own upload: 20 ms 0.88x).
WINIT 1 MiB (2 MiB kept echo 10x10 at 313 MB vs 130-165 MB).

Reviews: round 1 (reviewer A via transcript): P1 cap (honest pend <= PEND_HARD), P2 eager compat client pongs 40 ms late,
P3 impossibly early pongs do not touch est/window, min_rtt ~0. Round 2 (two seats; reviewer C 402): sweep liveness net
(+ test, mutation caught), pong re-grants at every level, flush staged upload before a pong, packets above the window,
cap at the largest packet; fuzz build fixed (credit + metrics modules, Worker::new stats arg; master's observe merge had
broken it), fuzz variant 1 runs --credit-v2. Fuzz replay 10/10 PASS.
Known limits (documented, not fixed): a browser answers pings before its JS handled the CONTINUEs (WHATWG), and a
client may switch to bigger packets: the bound can then be low by one earlier grant, so the cap is not a guarantee;
the hold stays the safety net. The compat eager case catches a missing probe only 1 of 3 runs; the unit test
grant_tops_up_to_the_window_and_probes_a_blocked_client is the deterministic guard.
Compat (permanent, wst compat): credit-v2 (tests/compat/credit_v2.py: pong / never pong / eager wisp-js pattern /
foreign pongs), wispjs-v2c, adv-v2c: PASS. epoxy simple-wisp-client v1/v2 upload OK (manual).

Why A5 fails: S1's window grows toward 56 MiB in its read phases; when its target stalls, the credit already out
(about a window) still crosses the one shared TCP connection and competes with S2. base-2 stops S1's refills at
PEND_LOW and S1 had less out. Options: (a) per-connection window budget (sum of a connection's windows <= WMAX);
(b) shrink a stream's window when its target stalls (backlog >= PEND_LOW for a whole pong interval); (c) accept
(A5's premise, the whole-connection hold, did not occur with either build).

Note on A5's premise (lead request): in no A5 run did base-2 show a whole-connection hold (0 holds, 0 dead seconds
for S2 in 8 runs x 3 builds, wbench client with a 96 MiB send buffer). The design review worry that base-2's hold freezes
sibling streams did not reproduce. A5 was then measured as written: S2's rate during S1's stalls, as a share of S2 alone.

### Option 2 (lead's pick, 8bf8c74): shrink a stalled stream's window
- In `Stream::on_pong`: if the stream has siblings on its connection, its target's backlog was >= PEND_LOW at every
  write since the last pong (`eased` = some write saw it below) and still is at the pong, the window halves (floor WINIT).
  It regrows by the rate rule (or starvation growth) once the target reads.
- Lone streams are exempt: in the delay-line sim (stalling sink, one stream) the shrink cut delivered packets 13x, and
  A1 (one stream, v2 1.45x base-2) is that case.
- The stream keeps its starvation-pool claim (`extra`) when it shrinks and regrows from it first. Handing the bytes
  back to the pool at once raised laptop echo 10x10 VmHWM by ~60 MB (5-way variant test); keeping them is neutral
  (ratio 0.99, n=6, p=0.69).
