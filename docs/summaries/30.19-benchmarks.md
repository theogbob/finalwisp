# 30.19 Benchmarks: finalwisp vs other Wisp servers

The numbers behind the README's performance table. Date: 2026-11-02 (big server) and 2026-11-06 (VPSes).

## Method
- Load generator: wbench (this repo, `bench/`), 5 s per cell after a 1 s warm-up, median of 3 runs, server order
  rotated between runs. Targets (echo, sink, source) run on the same machine; all traffic is loopback and plain `ws://` (no TLS).
- Cases: upload, download and echo with 1 stream or 10 connections x N streams; request/response (64 B) with 1 or
  10 x 10 streams; "churn" opens a new stream per request.
- Servers at their default or documented settings, loopback and private targets allowed (the targets are local):
  finalwisp base-6; epoxy-server (multithreaded runtime); wisp-server-python 0.9.0 (its default multi-process mode;
  memory = the whole process group); wisp-js 0.5.0 on Node 24 (one thread by design); wispurr with its author's
  speed configuration. None of them was tuned.
- On the 2-core VPSes the load generator and the targets share the 2 cores with the server, the same for every server.
- Kernel TCP memory pressure: a bulk cell during which the kernel entered TCP memory pressure is invalid (results
  under pressure are erratic for every server). On the 3.8 GB VPSes all 10 x 10 bulk cells hit it, so those rows were
  re-run at 10 x 2; cells still under pressure are marked and left out of the README ranges.
- Memory: "RSS after the case" = resident memory right after the case (largest case shown); "peak" = the process's
  lifetime high-water mark (VmHWM).

## Notes
- "fw x other" = finalwisp / other; higher means finalwisp is faster. Memory rows: other / finalwisp.
- wispurr moved no data in the single-stream upload and echo tests, was killed by the kernel's OOM killer in echo
  10 x 10 on VPS B (3.3-3.4 GB) and once on VPS A, and reached 9 streams/s in VPS A's churn test after that growth;
  the README leaves that churn cell out. The tested version has known data-loss and flow-control bugs.
- Echo 10 x 10 on VPS A (dropped cell): finalwisp 154 vs epoxy-server 332 MiB/s (8 paired runs). finalwisp itself
  drives this 3.8 GB box into TCP memory pressure: it takes uploads faster than the clients drain the echoed data, so
  the queue sits in kernel socket buffers, and under pressure the kernel shrinks the client send buffers. epoxy-server
  is CPU-bound earlier and stays out of pressure. When every server is under pressure (memory held from outside),
  finalwisp is 3.3x epoxy-server; at 10 x 5 it is 2.5x. A fix (stop refilling a stream's upload credit while its
  replies wait on the client) is planned; `--tcpmem-adapt` helps this case today (3.9x) but costs ~10 % at 10 x 5.
- Peak memory: on the 2-core VPSes finalwisp's peak (204-231 MiB) was about epoxy-server's (180-234) and 21-48 %
  above wisp-server-python's (156-168); on the big server it was the lowest (2-23x below the others).
- Loopback is not the internet: over a real network the link usually limits throughput long before the server does.
