## Where echo memory goes
Laptop, `--threads 2`, `wbench echo 10x10` 16 KiB, user scope, memory probes only (not timing). Base (6f6c402,
level 0): RSS 350-357 MiB, cgroup sock 400-510 MiB. In-process dump at the idle sweep: ~95% of the heap is
`Target::pend` (upload backlog in front of a target that does not read). Per stream: pend peak 4-5 MiB =
PEND_LOW 256 KiB + one 128-packet grant (2 MiB at 16 KiB) + what the client sent before the CONTINUE arrived.
Why: echo is a closed loop. The return path (target -> server -> client download) limits, the echo target stops
reading, its socket fills (the kernel already holds ~3 MiB per stream), and every refill lets the client add
another 2+ MiB that can only wait in pend. Throughput does not need it.
Ruled out (RSS median): free pend on drain 392 (glibc arena reuse, worse); MALLOC_MMAP_THRESHOLD_=256K + free 232
(mmap/munmap + zeroing per regrowth: CPU); TCP_NOTSENT_LOWAT on targets (kernel side only); grant <= 256 KiB for all
streams 80 (costs WAN upload: window/RTT). Full table: raw data (not published) (local).

## Wisp credit is absolute: overwrite and chain-merge collapse
A CONTINUE **sets** the client's credit to its value; it is not added (wisp-js `continue_received`: buffer_size =
value; epoxy: AtomicU32 store). Base refills at half of the grant. On a long path many CONTINUEs are in flight at
once, and each one resets the client to a full 128 packets: base reaches 150-330 MiB/s at 200 ms only because of
this accidental overlap. When two CONTINUEs reach the client back to back, the first one's remainder is
overwritten, the chains merge, and the stream can lock at --buffer packets per RTT (~10 MiB/s at 200 ms).
Laptop proxy rig (delay_proxy 100 ms each way, fast sink, 1x1; earlier worker's runs): base fell into it in 1 of 18
runs; a plain busy cut (smaller grants) in 1 of 3-5. In this session's loaded kill test, 3 of 10 base runs ended at
10-11 MiB/s (same signature).

## Ghost credit: why a hard window on allow - since starves long paths
Tried: never send a CONTINUE that raises `next_allow(allow, since, g)` above W (16 or 32 MiB). `allow - since` keeps
every unused remainder, including credit the client already overwrote (the server cannot tell when a CONTINUE was
received). Sim at 200 ms: at a refill the bound says 768 packets out, the true worst case is 333. Result
(credit_sim.py, 200 ms, client 300 MiB/s): W=16 MiB 24-35 MiB/s, W=32 MiB 39-49 (base 52-60 in the sim).
Rig (laptop proxy, 3 runs, rough): base 155 215 131 vs W=16 MiB 17 27 12 MiB/s. Not shipped.
The same sim shows base's `allow` itself has no limit (241 MiB after 4 s at 200 ms): in base PEND_HARD is the only
real cap, and a client that follows the protocol may legally use ghost credit.

## The rule (option 2, `Worker::grant_size`, level 0; under pressure the 20.26 stage grant still bounds it)
At each refill (unchanged gate: since >= half, backlog < PEND_LOW):
- **busy** = pend holds bytes, or a write came back short since the last grant (incl. flush_pending), or the
  target socket has >= 64 KiB unsent (SIOCOUTQNSD). pend > 0 -> floor at once: 256 KiB of credit (in packets of
  the last DATA size, >= 2). Otherwise an expanded grant shrinks by 1/4 (not below --buffer): a short burst does
  not drop a long-path stream to the floor and back into the collapse.
- **dry** (client-limited) = at the first upload write after a grant, pend is empty, < 64 KiB unsent, and >= 20 ms
  passed since the grant -> double the grant, up to EXPAND x --buffer (4 x 128 = 512 packets, 8 MiB at 16 KiB).
  Sampled once per grant (sticky): a target that just barely eats a grant empties at its end and would double
  falsely if any moment counted. The 20 ms gap: without it a stream whose next packet was already on the wire
  (it never waited for credit) would count as dry and expand on any timing, including loopback (mechanism reasoning,
  not separately measured). Cost: below ~20 ms RTT expansion does not trigger (not needed there: base
  rate already reaches the client's rate, sim 295 MiB/s at 20 ms).
- **otherwise hold** the current grant (>= --buffer). Going back to --buffer would let the next refill of the same
  burst overwrite an expansion at the client (absolute credit), i.e. the collapse above (mechanism reasoning, not
  separately measured).
- Refill at half of the current grant (capped at EXPAND x --buffer). `next_allow` uses the value actually sent;
  the model test (credit_allowance_catches_over_credit_after_refills) covers grants of 2..2048 packets.
EXPAND 16 was tried first: an honest 1x1 upload over the rig got CLOSE 0x49 in 1 of ~10 runs (pend over
PEND_HARD 64 MiB when the sink hiccupped). At 4: 0 errors in 10 runs (laptop, timing invalid).
Wire: CONTINUE > --buffer per stream. Checked live over the rig (100 ms each way): epoxy simple-wisp-client
received up to 384 (default --buffer 128; 1360 packets / 100 ms, no errors); wisp-js up to 128 with --buffer 32
(256 MiB delivered, > 4600 DATA packets per 200 ms, no errors). wbench sets credit = value.

## Known risk: honest-stream kill is statistical, as in base
Sim (credit_sim.py, 200 ms): the true worst-case outstanding credit reaches ~73 MiB with option 2 (EXPAND 4 and 16
alike: the overlap of refill-at-half chains, not EXPAND, dominates), vs ~44 MiB in base; PEND_HARD is 64 MiB.
A kill needs the target to stall while most of that lands. Not excluded; the real-box gate is 0 kills in a
long-RTT fast-sink upload x20. If it fails: raise PEND_HARD (e.g. 128 MiB; it only exists for clients that ignore
credit) or wait for (c).
Pre-existing in base (found 2026-10-23, VPS3, fw-mem3 = 6f6c402): base itself kills an honest stream. 1 Gbit /
200 ms netns link (tcp_rmem/wmem 64 MiB in the namespaces), wbench upload 1x1 into a sink that reads U(0.3,1.5) s and
then pauses U(0.5,2) s: 1 of 40 base runs got CLOSE 0x49 (VmHWM 67.6 MiB > PEND_HARD 64 MiB); base's 1x1 VmHWM median
61 MiB, so it runs at the edge of PEND_HARD. Fix later, not in 20.27 (likely: PEND_HARD relative to the measured
stream window instead of a constant).

## Future design (c): top-up window + CONTINUE-received tracking
Grant = W_s - true outstanding when the room is >= W_s/8, no refill at half: the bound equals the truth, so no
ghosts (sim: W=16 MiB 71 MiB/s, W=32 MiB 139 MiB/s at 200 ms; 297 at 20 ms). Needs (1) retiring credit the client
overwrote, i.e. knowing when a CONTINUE was received: a Wisp-path RTT (WS ping/pong through any proxy) and a small
per-stream ring of recent grants; (2) a per-stream W_s that grows from --buffer on a client-limited signal that
also works at short RTT (the 20 ms gap does not). Several hours plus a real long-path box: parked.
